Nairobi
The Green City in the Sun

Kenya's vibrant capital — where skyscrapers meet savannah and world-class culture thrives alongside wildlife.

Overview

Africa's Most Dynamic Capital

Nairobi is one of Africa's most exciting and rapidly evolving cities. Founded in 1899 as a railway depot on the Uganda Railway, it has grown into a thriving metropolis of over 5 million people and the economic, cultural, and political hub of East Africa. Known as the "Green City in the Sun," Nairobi enjoys a pleasant highland climate year-round and is blanketed with jacaranda and flame trees.

What makes Nairobi truly unique is its proximity to wilderness. It is the only capital city in the world that hosts a national park within its borders, and its suburbs are home to giraffes, zebras, and a wealth of birdlife. The city offers a compelling blend of modern skyscrapers, colonial-era architecture, vibrant markets, world-class restaurants, and a thriving arts scene.

Nairobi National Park

Wilderness at the City's Doorstep

Nairobi National Park is a remarkable 117-square-kilometer wildlife sanctuary located just 7 kilometers from the city center. The park is home to lions, leopards, cheetahs, hyenas, buffalo, giraffes, zebras, and over 400 bird species. Its backdrop of Nairobi's skyline creates a surreal juxtaposition of wild Africa and urban development.

The park is also an important rhino sanctuary, with one of Kenya's most successful black rhino conservation programs. A half-day game drive from the city is enough to experience the park's highlights, making it perfect for travelers with limited time.

Cultural Attractions

Museums, Markets & Heritage

The Nairobi National Museum offers a comprehensive journey through Kenya's natural and cultural heritage, featuring the famous "Cradle of Humankind" exhibits, including the Turkana Boy skeleton. Nearby, the Karen Blixen Museum preserves the home of the Out of Africa author. The Kazuri Beads Factory provides insight into local craftsmanship and supports women artisans.

The Maasai Market (rotating locations) offers authentic Maasai beadwork, wood carvings, and textiles. The Bomas of Kenya showcases traditional music and dance performances from many of Kenya's 42 tribes.

Dining & Nightlife

A Culinary Melting Pot

Nairobi's dining scene rivals any in Africa. From upscale restaurants in Westlands and Kilimani to vibrant street food in the city center, the city offers everything from traditional nyama choma (roasted meat) to international fine dining. The Alchemist and Brew Bistro represent the city's craft beer movement, while Talisman and Cultiva specialize in farm-to-table cuisine.

For nightlife, Westlands is the epicenter with clubs, lounges, and bars that stay open late. Jazz clubs and rooftop bars offer more relaxed options with stunning city views.

Day Trips

Beyond the City Limits

Nairobi serves as the perfect base for day trips to nearby attractions. The David Sheldrick Wildlife Trust offers a magical morning visit to see orphaned baby elephants being cared for. The Giraffe Centre lets you come face-to-face with endangered Rothschild's giraffes, while the nearby Sheldrick Elephant Orphanage is a heartwarming experience not to be missed.

The Great Rift Valley viewpoint at the edge of the city offers stunning panoramas, and the tea plantations of Kiambu and Limuru provide a peaceful escape into rolling green hills.

Getting Around

Navigating Nairobi

Nairobi has a range of transport options. Ride-hailing apps like Uber and Bolt are widely available and affordable. The Nairobi Expressway offers a fast link between the airport and the city center. For a local experience, matatus (minibuses) operate on fixed routes throughout the city. Car rental with a driver is recommended for first-time visitors. Jomo Kenyatta International Airport is one of Africa's busiest hubs with flights to major cities worldwide.
