Amboseli
Giants Beneath the Peak

Home to Africa's most magnificent elephant herds against the stunning backdrop of Mount Kilimanjaro.

Overview

A Landscape of Legends

Amboseli National Park is one of Kenya's most iconic safari destinations, renowned for its massive elephant herds and breathtaking views of Mount Kilimanjaro � Africa's highest peak. Spanning 392 square kilometers in southern Kenya near the Tanzanian border, Amboseli offers a unique landscape of dried-up lake beds, sulfur springs, savannah grasslands, and acacia woodlands.

The park is part of a larger ecosystem that extends beyond its boundaries, with wildlife moving freely through surrounding community conservancies. Amboseli's open plains make wildlife viewing exceptional throughout the year, and the park is particularly famous for the quality of its light � photographers flock here for golden-hour shots of elephants silhouetted against Kilimanjaro.

Elephant Heritage

The Lords of Amboseli

Amboseli is home to over 1,500 African elephants, making it one of the best places on Earth to observe these gentle giants up close. The Amboseli Elephant Research Project, founded by Dr. Cynthia Moss in 1972, is the longest-running study of wild elephants in the world, and has provided invaluable insights into elephant behavior, social structures, and communication.

The elephants of Amboseli are remarkably habituated, allowing for intimate viewing experiences at close range. Watching a family of elephants cross the plains with Kilimanjaro rising in the background is one of the most iconic images in all of African travel. The park's marshes and swamps provide vital water sources that sustain these herds through the dry season.

Views of Kilimanjaro

Africa's Rooftop

Mount Kilimanjaro, at 5,895 meters, dominates the Amboseli skyline. The mountain's snow-capped peak � though its glaciers are receding � provides a spectacular backdrop that makes Amboseli's landscapes truly unforgettable. The best views are at sunrise and sunset when the mountain is most visible, often with a halo of clouds encircling its summit.

Observation Hill, located within the park, offers panoramic views of the entire Amboseli ecosystem with Kilimanjaro as the centerpiece. On clear days, the mountain appears close enough to touch, and photographers consider Amboseli the best place in Africa for Kilimanjaro photography.

Wildlife & Birdlife

Beyond the Elephants

While elephants are the main attraction, Amboseli supports a rich diversity of wildlife. Lions, cheetahs, hyenas, giraffes, zebras, wildebeests, and impalas are commonly seen. The park is also home to over 400 species of birds, including pelicans, kingfishers, crakes, hamerkops, and the stunning Maasai ostrich.

The park's wetlands attract water birds in large numbers, while the dry plains host bustards, secretary birds, and vultures. Amboseli's birdlife is at its peak during the wet season when migratory species arrive from Europe and northern Africa.

Best Time to Visit

When to Go

Dry Season (June � October & January � February): Wildlife congregates around water sources, making viewing exceptional. Clear skies offer the best Kilimanjaro views. This is peak tourist season.

Wet Season (March � May & November � December): The park turns lush and green. Bird watching is superb, and rates are lower. Some roads may become challenging.

For the best experience, plan your visit during the dry months when wildlife is most concentrated and Kilimanjaro is most visible.

Getting There

How to Reach Amboseli

Amboseli is approximately a 4-hour drive from Nairobi via the Mombasa Highway (A109) and C103. Scheduled flights from Wilson Airport land at Amboseli Airstrip inside the park, taking about 40 minutes. Most safari operators include transfers in their packages. A 4x4 vehicle is recommended for game drives within the park.
