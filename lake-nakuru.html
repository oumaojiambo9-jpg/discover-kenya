Lake Nakuru
A Rift Valley Gem

A dazzling soda lake famed for its flamingos, a thriving rhino sanctuary, and breathtaking Rift Valley scenery.

Overview

Nature's Masterpiece in the Rift Valley

Lake Nakuru National Park is one of Kenya's most unique and beautiful wildlife destinations. Centered around a shallow alkaline lake in the Great Rift Valley, the park covers 188 square kilometers and is famous for its shimmering pink shores covered by thousands of flamingos. The lake itself sits at an elevation of 1,759 meters and is fed by three main rivers: the Njoro, Makalia, and Nderit.

The park was established in 1961 and was designated a UNESCO Ramsar site in 1990. Its alkaline waters support an abundance of cyanobacteria and algae, which attract vast numbers of both lesser and greater flamingos, making Lake Nakuru one of the world's great ornithological spectacles.

The Flamingos

A Sea of Pink

The sight of hundreds of thousands of flamingos feeding along the shores of Lake Nakuru is one of Africa's most unforgettable experiences. The flamingos' pink color comes from the beta-carotene in the algae and cyanobacteria they consume. The lesser flamingo is the more numerous species, easily identified by its deeper crimson bill and darker pink plumage.

Flamingo numbers fluctuate with water levels and food availability. When conditions are optimal, the lake can host over 1 million flamingos, creating a pink shoreline that stretches as far as the eye can see. The best viewing is often during the dry season when water levels are lower and algae concentrations are higher.

Rhino Sanctuary

Protecting Endangered Giants

Lake Nakuru is one of Kenya's most important rhino sanctuaries, with significant populations of both black and white rhinos protected within the park's electric fence. The park was established as a rhino sanctuary in 1987 and has been instrumental in the recovery of the critically endangered eastern black rhino.

The park's fenced boundary provides a safe haven where rhinos can breed and thrive without the threat of poaching. KWS rangers patrol the sanctuary around the clock, and the success of the program has allowed some rhinos to be translocated to other parks, helping to establish new populations across Kenya.

Other Wildlife

Beyond the Lake

While flamingos and rhinos are the headline attractions, Lake Nakuru supports a diverse range of wildlife including lions, leopards, buffaloes, Rothschild's giraffes, zebras, impalas, and waterbucks. The park's woodlands and acacia forests provide habitat for colobus monkeys, baboons, and the elusive serval cat.

The park also has a healthy population of pythons, including the famous "Python Hill" area where visitors can sometimes see these massive snakes basking in the sun. The Makalia Falls area is a popular picnic spot and offers excellent game viewing.

Bird Watching

A Birder's Paradise

Lake Nakuru is a world-class birding destination with over 450 species recorded. Beyond flamingos, the lake attracts pelicans, cormorants, herons, storks, avocets, and plovers. The surrounding woodlands host the stunning African fish eagle, grey-headed kingfisher, and the colorful lilac-breasted roller.

The best birding is along the lake shore and at the river inlets where the concentration of water birds is highest. Early morning game drives offer the most active birdlife, with many species feeding after sunrise.
