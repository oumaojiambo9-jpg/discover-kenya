📜 Visas & Entry

Visas & Entry
Requirements & Procedures

Do I need a visa to visit Kenya?

Yes, most nationalities require an approved eTA (Electronic Travel Authorization) before traveling to Kenya. The application is completed online at the official Kenya eTA portal. Citizens of some African countries are exempt. Apply at least 3 business days before departure.

How long can I stay in Kenya on an eTA?

The standard eTA allows a stay of up to 90 days for tourism or business purposes. Extensions can be applied for at the Department of Immigration in Nairobi if you wish to stay longer.

What documents do I need for the eTA application?

You need a valid passport (6+ months validity), a recent passport photo, a scanned copy of your passport bio-data page, travel itinerary, accommodation details, and a credit/debit card for the fee.

Can I get a visa on arrival at JKIA?

No, Kenya no longer offers visas on arrival. All travelers must obtain an approved eTA before departure. Airlines check for eTA approval before issuing boarding passes.

🚑️ Health & Safety

Health & Safety
Staying Well in Kenya

Do I need vaccinations to travel to Kenya?

Yellow Fever vaccination is required if you are arriving from a country with risk of yellow fever transmission. Routine vaccines (MMR, diphtheria-tetanus-pertussis, polio) should be up to date. Typhoid, Hepatitis A & B, and cholera vaccines are recommended. Consult your doctor 6-8 weeks before travel.

Is malaria a concern in Kenya?

Yes, malaria is present in most regions of Kenya below 2,500m elevation, including Nairobi and all safari areas. Malaria prophylaxis is strongly recommended. Use mosquito repellent with DEET, sleep under mosquito nets, and take anti-malarial medication as prescribed.

Is Kenya safe for tourists?

Yes, Kenya is generally safe for tourists who exercise common sense. Safari areas and tourist resorts are well-protected. In cities, avoid walking alone at night, keep valuables out of sight, and use reputable taxis. The tourism police have a dedicated hotline for visitors.

Do I need travel insurance?

Travel insurance is strongly recommended. Ensure your policy covers medical evacuation, safari activities, trip cancellation, and lost luggage. Medical evacuation insurance is especially important for remote safari areas.

💰 Money & Costs

Money & Costs
Currency & Budgeting

What currency is used in Kenya?

The Kenyan Shilling (KES) is the local currency. As of 2026, $1 USD is approximately 145 KES. Major hotels and lodges accept US dollars and credit cards, but local markets and small businesses prefer KES cash.

Are credit cards widely accepted?

Visa and Mastercard are accepted at most hotels, lodges, upscale restaurants, and larger shops. American Express has limited acceptance. ATMs are widely available in cities. Carry cash for remote areas and park fees.

How much does a safari cost?

Safari costs vary widely. Budget camping safaris start from $150-200 per person per day. Mid-range lodge safaris range from $300-500 per person per day. Luxury safaris cost $800-2,500+ per person per day. Park entry fees are additional ($60-80 per day).

🚶 Getting Around

Getting Around
Transport in Kenya

What is the best way to travel between parks?

Domestic flights are the most efficient way to travel between Kenya's major parks and reserves. Airlines like Safarilink and AirKenya offer regular flights from Wilson Airport to airstrips throughout the country. Driving between parks is possible but can take several hours on unpaved roads.

Can I drive myself on safari?

Self-driving is possible and popular with independent travelers. A 4x4 vehicle is essential. Major parks have well-marked roads and gates. However, a guided safari offers better wildlife spotting and local knowledge. Remember to drive on the left side of the road.

Are ride-sharing apps available in Kenya?

Yes, Uber and Bolt operate in Nairobi, Mombasa, and other major towns. They are reliable, affordable, and safer than hailing unknown taxis on the street. Fares are paid through the app and no cash is needed.

🏝 Safari Tips

Safari Tips
Making the Most of Your Safari

What should I wear on safari?

Wear neutral-colored clothing (khaki, olive, beige, tan). Avoid dark blue and black which attract tsetse flies. Dress in layers � mornings are cool, afternoons are hot. Comfortable walking shoes, a wide-brimmed hat, and sunglasses are essential.

What camera gear should I bring?

A DSLR or mirrorless camera with a zoom lens of 200-400mm is ideal for wildlife photography. A 70-200mm lens works well for most situations. Bring extra memory cards and batteries (charging opportunities are limited at many camps).

What is the best time of day for game drives?

Early morning (6:00-9:00 AM) and late afternoon (4:00-6:30 PM) are the best times for game viewing. Animals are most active during these cooler periods. Mid-day is typically quiet, with animals resting in the shade.

Can children go on safari?

Yes, Kenya is a family-friendly safari destination. Many lodges and camps welcome children and offer specialized programs. Children under 12 often receive discounted rates. Some camps have age restrictions (minimum 6 or 8 years) due to wildlife safety.

🌎 General

General Questions
Quick Answers

What language is spoken in Kenya?

English and Swahili (Kiswahili) are the official languages. English is widely spoken in tourist areas, hotels, and by guides. Learning a few Swahili phrases like "Jambo" (hello) and "Asante" (thank you) is appreciated by locals.

Do I need to tip in Kenya?

Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory. Standard guidelines: safari guide $10-20 per day per group, lodge/restaurant staff $2-5 per day, restaurant service 10% if not included. Some lodges include a service charge in their rates.

What is the mobile phone coverage like?

Mobile coverage is excellent in cities and towns. Most safari lodges and camps have wifi, though speeds vary. In remote parks, coverage may be limited to 3G or be unavailable. Safaricom offers the best coverage nationwide. You can buy a local SIM at the airport.
